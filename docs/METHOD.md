# Methods

## Question and target

Each row is a current original 10-K for a nonfinancial US firm. The target is the following original 10-K's full-year reported property, plant and equipment cash payments divided by its full-year consolidated revenue. This is realized spending intensity, not a measure of the accuracy of a manager's published guidance or total cash allocation. Dividends, repurchases and R&D use different accounting concepts; they are separate descriptive targets when original-filing tags exist.

The pinned universe comprises 50 retail and 50 manufacturing firms. The panel requires both source and next original annual filings. CNH has no eligible paired observation, leaving 99 firms and 1,163 rows. Revenue and capex require annual USD facts with matching source accession, report end and 330–400-day period. Where conflicting consolidated revenue tags coexist, the larger positive candidate is chosen; this is a documented heuristic rather than a full semantic reconciliation. The exact tag and original accession are recorded for review. Thirty-two sampled source facts reconcile to the original inline XBRL; this check is bounded to eight firms and cannot validate every row.

Selected excerpt candidates match investment, buyback, dividend and research terms in normalized visible filing HTML. A deterministic score prefers forward-looking and numerical language; at most four capex and two other excerpts are kept. Evidence offsets refer to the normalized visible text. The frozen encoder uses up to four 256-token chunks per document with attention-mask token means then equal chunk means; the audit file records document/chunk counts and truncation. The model does not read the full filing and the excerpt may contain boilerplate.

## Availability and evaluation

The decision time is the **source filing date**. The outcome exists for learning only after the **following filing date**. Training labels have `target_known_at < 2022-01-01`. Validation forecasts have `2022-01-01 <= known_at < 2023-01-01`, with validation labels published before 2024. Test forecasts have `2024-01-01 <= known_at < 2026-01-01`. This leaves a buffer for published validation outcomes. A lag is calculated only if the prior fiscal end is 330–400 days earlier and its filing predates the current filing. Missing lags remain missing and are imputed inside training-fitted preprocessing. Later target figures, accession identifiers and filing dates are never model features.

The direct ablation compares numeric residual ridge with numeric residual ridge plus frozen FinBERT embeddings on **identical** text-covered test IDs. Ridge penalties are selected by validation MAE. A separate comparison uses the best numbers-only benchmark selected on validation; its selection is documented rather than hidden. Other baselines include source-year persistence, sector median, standard ridge, histograms of gradient boosting, TF-IDF ridge and a three-seed GRU with only available annual source filings. The text encoder's weights are frozen. The finite-sample validation residual interval is a *diagnostic* because validation also selected hyperparameters and distribution shifts can invalidate coverage.

The paired statistic is mean absolute-error reduction, in percentage points. A 20,000-replicate company-cluster bootstrap draws companies with all their test rows and gives a percentile interval. A separate company sign-flip randomization computes a two-sided exploratory p-value. Shared year shocks, limited text firms and prior inspection of test results prevent confirmatory inference. The test period contains recurring firms; an additional 20-company numerical holdout checks a different kind of generalization. It does not establish text generalization to unseen firms.

## Limitations and provenance

The original fiscal-year protocol allowed some unpublished training and validation labels. The corrected outputs are a new audited revision, not a prospective pre-registered test; see [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md). The pretrained FinBERT corpus has not been independently proven to precede every forecast date, so the textual result cannot claim complete time isolation of encoder training. SEC HTML was intentionally cached for 24 firms and is a selective subset. The source snapshot is fixed; a fresh SEC pull can change facts, accessions and rows. Excerpt selection may miss relevant paragraphs, and financial tags can change meaning with acquisitions or reporting scope. Extreme errors need manual filing review before any operational use. None of the results imply deception, causal language effects or investment performance.
